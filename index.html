About Us

We are a group of five students from the Colegio San José from Valladolid, Spain. Eqolibrium was born as a project of our Information and Communication Technologies class and was intended to participate in the young entrepreneurship contest Gigas for Schools.

This was not our first idea, in fact, it was not our idea to act initially in the field of environmentalism, but in that of health.
However, we did not find a way to innovate as we wanted in this sector, and in the end we opted to help the fight for the climate.
But... how could we do something different in such a busy environment? Well, after conducting interviews with people around us and an environmental group in our city, we found that there was a somewhat aggressive and politicized vision of this movement.
That is why our motto "The friendly hand of environmentalism", we wanted to offer a kinder and more conciliatory vision of this initiative. Then came the other problem: how to make our message visual and effective? To achieve this, we decided to use the EF, ecological footprint, since it seems to us a very illustrative concept of the impact that the actions of each of us have on our planet. In addition, and following our motto, we not only wanted to show the problems caused by our users, we wanted to offer them viable and customized solutions. As we wanted to be more than a questionnaire, we decided to act more actively, through the dnations, and sharing Updates you can trust

What is Ecological Footprint?


The ecological footprint, also known as the environmental footprint, is a way of measuring the impact that humanity has on the planet. It is the ecologically productive surface necessary to produce the resources consumed by an individual, as well as that necessary to absorb the waste it generates.

Planned obsolescence

What is Scheduled Obsolescence?

Planned obsolescence is the useful life that a factory or company gives to a product, when this useful life period passes, the product will become obsolete, useless. This was created so that the consumer would be forced to purchase the same or similar new product. Most products are “programmed to die.” Many times when these devices die it is cheaper to acquire a new one than to repair the one we already have.
